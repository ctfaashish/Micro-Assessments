5. What is an internet exchange points (IXPs) and a peering link? (4 Points)
A physical location where Internet infrastructure organizations such as Internet Service
Providers (ISPs) and Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) connect with one another is known
as an Internet exchange point (IXP). These locations are on several networks’ edges, allowing
network providers to share transit outside of their own. By establishing a presence inside an
IXP site, companies may decrease their path to transit from other participating networks,
cutting latency, improving round-trip time, and perhaps saving costs.
The practice of connecting and exchanging traffic between two Internet networks is known as
peering. It allows them to send traffic across each other's customers without having to pay a
third party to do so on their behalf through the Internet.

9. What is traffic intensity? Describe the equation used to measure traffic intensity? How
is the traffic intensity characterized as small, large, or infinite? (7 Points)
The average occupancy of a server or resource for a specific period, usually a busy hour, is
referred to as traffic intensity which happens in telecommunications. Telecommunications
companies are keenly interested in traffic intensity since it determines how much equipment
they need to control the traffic.
It is measured with the help of Link bandwidth (R), Packet length (L), average packet arrival
rate(a). Therefore,
Traffic intensity = La/R.
If La/R is approximately equal to 0 then traffic intensity is considered small.
If La/R is less than or equals to 1 then traffic intensity is considered large.
If La/R is greater than 1 then traffic intensity is considered infinite.
(What is traffic intensity: 2 Point; Formula: 2 Point; Characterization: 3 Points; Total
= (2 + 2 + 3) = 7 Points)

11. Define latency, throughput, instantaneous throughput, and average throughput. In
what scenario (at least one example) is instantaneous throughput and average
throughput important? (10 Points)
Latency is defined as the time it takes for a packet to get from source to destination. It is
measured in units of time. (s, ms).
Throughput is defined as actual rate at which bits are transferred between sender and receiver.
Throughput is measured in kilobits per second, megabits per second, or gigabits per second.
Instantaneous throughput defined as the rate at which the bits are transferred at given point
of time between sender and receiver.
Average throughput is defined as the rate at which the bits are transferred over longer period
of time between sender and receiver.
Example for instantaneous throughput is streaming application and for average throughput it
is downloading the files.
(Each definition: 2 points; example explaining the scenario: 2 points; Total = ((4 * 2) + 2
= 10 points)
