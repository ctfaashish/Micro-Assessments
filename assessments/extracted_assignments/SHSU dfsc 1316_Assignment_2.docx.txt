7. Explain the function of the following fields in TCP header of a data packet: source and destination
port numbers, sequence number, and acknowledgement number (10 points).
Source and Destination Port Numbers—The communication endpoints for sending and receiving
devices
Sequence Number—Mark the ordering of a group of messages
Acknowledgement Number—Both senders and receivers use the acknowledgment numbers field to
communicate the sequence numbers of messages that are either recently received or expected to be
sent

13. What are the input and output of the Address Resolution Protocol (ARP)? What are the Windows
commands to show the current ARP cache table and to delete an entry with a specific IP address? (10
points)
The input of ARP is the IP Address and the output of the ARP is the MAC address. The windows
command to show the current ARP is “-a” and to delete the entry with a specified IP address is
“-d inet_addr”
14. Please briefly describe ARP cache poisoning. (5 points)
ARP poisoning is where a target computer could be convinced to send frames destined for one
computer to a different computer
15. What are the two most popular uses of IP spoofing? (5 points)
The two most popular used of IP spoofing include in Distributed Denial of Service attacks (DDoS)
and to break IP address-based authentication.
16. What are the possible defenses against DoS attacks? (5 points)
The possible defenses include not clicking on unknown websites or URLs from emails
17. What is SSL? Is it useful to prevent Web spoofing? Why? (10 points)
SSL stands for Secure Socket Layer protocol and was created by Netscape to ensure secure
transactions between web servers and browsers. The protocol uses a third party, a Certificate
Authority (CA), to identify one end or both ends of the transactions.
SSL connections use an encryption key and digital certificate to verify that
a website's communications originate from a reliable source. Though there are many reasons to
use SSL connections, SSL also help stop attackers from “spoofing.”
